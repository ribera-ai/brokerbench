# Key Findings: BrokerBench vs. the RE Broker Exam

## The Official Broker Exam (Pearson VUE, Effective Aug 2024)

The national/general portion of the Broker exam is **80 scored items + 5 unscored pretest items**, administered by Pearson VUE (ARELLO-accredited).

### Exam Item Distribution

| # | Topic Area | Items | Knowledge | Application | Analysis | % of Exam |
|---|-----------|------:|----------:|------------:|---------:|----------:|
| I | Real Property Characteristics, Legal Descriptions & Property Use | 10 | 2 | 5 | 3 | 12.5% |
| II | Forms of Ownership, Transfer & Recording of Title | 8 | 2 | 3 | 3 | 10.0% |
| III | Property Value & Appraisal | 10 | 2 | 4 | 4 | 12.5% |
| IV | Real Estate Contracts & Agency | 15 | 3 | 6 | 6 | 18.75% |
| V | Real Estate Practice (incl. Fair Housing & Risk Mgmt) | 12 | 5 | 5 | 2 | 15.0% |
| VI | Property Disclosures & Environmental Issues | 9 | 4 | 3 | 2 | 11.25% |
| VII | Financing & Settlement | 8 | 3 | 3 | 2 | 10.0% |
| VIII | Real Estate Math Calculations | 8 | 0 | 3 | 5 | 10.0% |
| | **Total** | **80** | **21** | **32** | **27** | **100%** |

### Cognitive Level Distribution (Broker vs. Salesperson)

| Cognitive Level | Broker Exam | Salesperson Exam | Difference |
|----------------|------------:|-----------------:|------------|
| **Knowledge** (recall facts) | 21 (26%) | 38 (48%) | Broker has far less pure recall |
| **Application** (use in situation) | 32 (40%) | 24 (30%) | Broker has more applied scenarios |
| **Analysis** (examine fact pattern, decide) | 27 (34%) | 18 (22%) | Broker has much more analysis |
| **Total** | 80 | 80 | |

**Key takeaway**: The Broker exam is dramatically harder — 74% of questions require Application or Analysis vs. 52% on the Salesperson exam. Less memorization, more judgment.

## What BrokerBench Tests That the Exam Doesn't

The exam tests whether you *know the rules*. BrokerBench tests whether you can *do the job*.

| Capability | On the Exam? | In BrokerBench? | Why It Matters |
|-----------|:------------:|:---------------:|----------------|
| Orchestrate a full transaction timeline | No | Yes (Domain 2) | The #1 thing brokers do daily |
| Write a compelling MLS listing | No | Yes (Domain 6) | Directly impacts sale price and days on market |
| Advise a first-time buyer on affordability | No | Yes (Domain 8) | Core client service |
| Manage trust account reconciliation | Barely (1-2 items) | Yes (Domain 7) | #1 reason brokers lose licenses |
| Negotiate repair requests from inspection | No | Yes (Domain 2) | Happens on ~80% of transactions |
| Identify wire fraud/BEC attacks | No | Yes (Domain 3) | $350M+ lost annually in RE wire fraud |
| Create Fair Housing-compliant ad copy | Indirectly | Yes (Domain 6) | Real enforcement scenarios |
| Calculate DTI with complex income | 1-2 items | 30+ instances (Domain 4) | Needed for every buyer client |
| Evaluate competing offers | No | Yes (Domain 2) | Common scenario in hot markets |
| Draft counter-offer strategy | No | Yes (Domain 5) | Real negotiation skill |

The exam caps at Analysis. BrokerBench adds an **Expert** tier for scenarios that would stump even experienced brokers: conflicting regulations, novel fact patterns, multi-jurisdictional edge cases.

## The Exam's Blind Spots

The Broker exam was designed to test whether a *human* has the baseline knowledge to practice. It does NOT test:

1. **Generation ability** (can you produce, not just recognize?)
   - Write a listing description
   - Draft a counter-offer
   - Create a CMA presentation
   - Compose a client advisory email

2. **Workflow ability** (can you orchestrate, not just understand?)
   - Manage overlapping deadlines across concurrent transactions
   - Coordinate between lender, title, inspector, and appraiser
   - Sequence the right actions when a deal starts falling apart

3. **Nuance ability** (can you handle gray areas?)
   - "Is this steering or honest neighborhood guidance?"
   - "Should I recommend waiving the appraisal contingency?"
   - "How do I handle a dual agency situation ethically?"

4. **Communication ability** (can you explain, not just know?)
   - Explain closing costs to a confused buyer
   - Break down a complex settlement statement
   - Counsel a seller on a below-asking offer

**BrokerBench tests all four.** The exam only tests recognition and partially analysis-level MCQ.

## Summary

| Dimension | Broker Exam | BrokerBench |
|-----------|:----------:|:-----------:|
| Total items | 80 | 1,500+ |
| Domains | 8 topics (single exam) | 8 domains (comprehensive suite) |
| Cognitive levels | 3 (K / App / An) | 4 (+ Expert) |
| Item formats | MCQ only | 6+ formats |
| Scope | Knowledge gate (can you practice?) | Performance benchmark (how good are you?) |
| Updates | Revised every few years | Version-controlled, continuously expanded |
| State coverage | Per-state supplements | National + 4 state supplements planned |

**Bottom line**: The Broker exam is an 80-question knowledge gate. BrokerBench is a 1,500+ instance performance benchmark. The exam asks "do you know enough to get a license?" BrokerBench asks "how well can you actually do the job?" The ideal collection covers everything on the exam at 6-7x the depth, plus the practical skills the exam doesn't test at all.
